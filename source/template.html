                    From a dish of cells to an answer

                    The data come from a large lab screen that grew cancer cells, dosed them with drugs and measured how much each dose slowed their growth.

                    1. 1

                      Grow the cells

                      984 cancer cell lines from 41 cancer types, each kept alive in the lab.

                    2. 2

                      Add the drug

                      Each line gets the drug at 5 or 9 doses for 72 hours.

                    3. 3

                      Find the dose that halves growth

                      That dose is the IC50. A lower IC50 means the drug works at a smaller dose.

                    4. 4

                      Compare two groups

                      Lines with the gene change against lines without it, inside the cancer type you picked.

                    How to read the answer

                    • Each dot is one cell line. Orange dots have the gene change.
                    • Grey-blue dots do not have it.
                    • Hollow dots never dropped to half their growth, even at the highest dose tested, so their IC50 is an estimate.
                    • The dark line in each row marks the median, the middle cell line of that group.

                    Further left on the chart means less drug was needed, so the drug worked better on those cells.

                    When the tool says yes

                    Every answer follows the same rule. The p-value is the chance of seeing a gap this big if the gene change made no difference.

                    AnswerWhat it takes
                    Gene change predicts sensitivityLines with the gene change need at least 2 times less drug, and p is below 0.05
                    Gene change predicts resistanceLines with the gene change need at least 2 times more drug, and p is below 0.05
                    Small differencep is below 0.05, but the gap is smaller than 2 times
                    No clear linkp is 0.05 or higher
                    Not enough cell linesEither group has fewer than 3 lines, so the tool gives no answer

                    The menus on the Ask page warn you ahead of time. A green check means the choice leaves at least 3 lines on each side, given your other two picks. A red cross means the tool would give no answer.

                    Ranking many drugs at once

                    The Rank drugs page runs the same comparison for every drug, or for every gene change, in one go. Testing hundreds of pairs means some will pass p below 0.05 by luck, so the ranking uses a stricter cutoff, a false discovery rate below 5% (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995).

                    • Clear link. Passes the stricter cutoff and shows a gap of at least 2 times.
                    • Possible link. Shows a gap of at least 2 times with p below 0.05, but does not pass the stricter cutoff, so it could be luck.

                    The menus on this page mark each choice too. A green check shows how many drugs or gene changes that choice leaves to rank. A red cross means none of them has at least 3 lines on each side, so the list would come back empty.

                    Where the data come from

                    Every number comes from GDSC1000 release v17, a public dataset from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer project at the Wellcome Sanger Institute and Massachusetts General Hospital (Iorio et al. 2016). The tool uses 210,186 results: 250 drugs tested on 984 cancer cell lines, with 397 gene changes recorded for each line.

                    The files come straight from the GDSC team. Drug responses come from gdscdata, gene changes and cell line names from gdsctools, and drug names and targets from cancerrxgene.org. GDSC makes these data free for academic use.

                    Limits

                    • Cell lines are not patients. They grow on plastic without blood vessels, immune cells or neighboring tissue, and many have changed over years in culture.
                    • A gene is recorded only as changed or not. The data do not say which exact mutation a line carries, so BRAF V600E is counted together with other cancer-linked BRAF mutations.
                    • Not every drug was tested on every cell line, so some groups are small or empty.
                    • About two thirds of IC50 values lie past the highest dose tested, so they are estimates. The test uses only the order of the values, which keeps those estimates from driving the result.
                    • Try enough combinations and some will pass the 0.05 cutoff by luck. The Rank drugs page corrects for this, but single questions on the Ask page do not, so treat a surprising result as a lead to confirm.
                    The statistics, for the curious
                    1. Test. A two-sided Mann-Whitney U test on log IC50 (Mann and Whitney 1947). It is exact for small groups and uses the normal approximation with tie and continuity corrections for large ones.
                    2. Typical fold difference. The median IC50 ratio over every pair made of one line with the gene change and one line without it, the Hodges-Lehmann estimate (Hodges and Lehmann 1963). Its 95% range comes from inverting the same test (Bauer 1972), so the range and the p-value tell the same story.
                    3. Pooled cancer types. A model with a separate average for each cancer type plus one gene-change effect, tested with a t-test. This is the same idea as the analysis in Iorio et al. (2016).
                    4. Other gene changes. For a single cancer type, the tool checks every other gene change for an uneven split between the two groups (Fisher exact test) and flags the strongest one when p is below 0.001.
                    5. Repeat screens. Fifteen drugs were screened twice under different IDs. The tool runs the same comparison on the second screen and says whether the two agree.
                    6. Data cleaning. Of 222,339 fitted curves, 807 with a poor fit (RMSE above 0.3) were removed, the same rule GDSC uses for its releases. For 24 drugs screened at two dose ranges, the tool kept the range used on most lines, which removed 571 curves. Lines without genetic data were removed, which leaves 210,186 results on 984 cell lines.
                    7. Cancer types. Each line takes the TCGA cancer type that GDSC assigns. The 180 lines without one are grouped by their GDSC tissue label, for example Ewing sarcoma.
                    8. Ranking. Each drug (or gene change) with at least 3 lines on each side is tested as above. The p-values are then adjusted with the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate. Pooled cancer types use the within-type model.
                    9. Code checks. The statistics match SciPy and an exact calculation of the Mann-Whitney distribution to at least 6 significant digits.
